    body: |-
      Visual research and diploma project at the HSE School of Design, tutor — Ilya Bannikov-Brumm, 2025. Explainer, 3D and author’s animation.

      Why do some simple things suddenly start to feel meaningful? Why can a piece of butter, a dish or a gesture evoke a sense of value, as if something more were hidden inside? We meet this every day in art, advertising and everyday life, but rarely think about how exactly the feeling arises.

      This explainer tells how an aura of presence and emotional depth forms around an ordinary object. Where it comes from is not obvious. The answer appears gradually, through the play of light, arrangement and attention that bring things to life.

  prologue:
    label: Prologue
    body: |-
      Imagine that everything begins with a table. An ordinary wooden table, warm from the soft light of a lamp. It is dark all around, only this circle of light, like a stage, like a world in miniature.

      Objects lie on it — a sheet of paper, a spoon, a glint of glass, a wrapper, a scrap of a drawing, a piece of cloth. They look like a jumbled puzzle with no beginning and no end. But if you look closer, you will notice that something is happening between them. The light slowly moves, and the things seem to come alive.
  light:
    label: Act I. Light
    body: |-
      Sometimes it seems that this is exactly how meaning is born: from an attentive gaze, from slow light, from a gesture that asks for nothing.

      Light is the first gesture of faith. It makes a thing visible and at the same time out of reach. In 17th-century paintings it fell evenly, dividing the world into categories: the rich and the simple, bread and butter, glass and cloth. Even then the artist understood that what matters is not the object itself but how it is depicted.

      In Chardin’s work light became quiet and domestic, with no luxury in it, only calm and attention. In Vollon’s, a piece of butter shone like a monument, the light living inside it, as if the substance itself kept the warmth of a hand.

      Today this light has returned — on screens, in shop windows, in advertising videos and social media still lifes. Soft, diffused, shadowless… It promises us authenticity, yet always stays slightly out of reach.
  vessel:
    label: Act II. Vessel
    body: |-
      Light reveals a thing while keeping its mystery. If light makes a thing visible, the vessel teaches it to be kept.

      The vessel was once the body of a ritual. In Sakha culture it is the choron, a wooden goblet in which milk and butter turned into offerings to the gods. Everything placed inside acquired special meaning. Form gave meaning, and the boundaries of the vessel turned the accidental into the valuable and the kept.
  vessel2:
    body: |-
      Later came glass butter dishes, porcelain moulds and paper wrappers. These are different vessels, yet the same. Their task is to separate, preserve and emphasise. The white surface of paper, the smoothness of glass — all of it continues an ancient faith in the frame.
  vessel3:
    body: |-
      Today packaging plays the same role. It makes a thing special by creating a space of respect around it. We unwrap it like an altar, we feel the moment of access — and that moment matters more than the contents.
