    body: |-
      Visual research, HSE School of Design, 2024.

      Husky is a musician from Ulan-Ude with a whip-sharp word, an original flow and a style of his own. His concerts are conceptual performances that never repeat. His lyrics touch on difficult and sometimes grim themes, his songs carry ideas, and his metaphors make the words slightly blurred yet alluring and sweet. A new track usually comes out with a video, which makes his work even more interesting. The videos were shot by different directors, and Husky himself often took the camera, releasing short films and video works.

  concept:
    label: Concept
    body: |-
      Everything shot by Husky or for Husky — from music videos to short and documentary films — usually conveys the same image: an uneducated, distinctive musician from the provinces, a Yesenin or Mayakovsky of our time (depending on the commentator). Different images and discourses intertwine in his work. Panel blocks and the theme of “Russian chthonic” meet the natural landscapes of Ulan-Ude. References to the past echo the construction of a possible future; the documentary sits next to the surreal.

      — If not for music, what would you be doing?

      — Documentary film. It’s the place with the least reality, where there is none at all.

      “Husky answers 33 questions”, Vogue Russia, 2019

      Most of the works show not only Husky’s outward image but also his inner world. The same devices, images and themes recur, and the universe of his videos feels whole and connected.

      At first it was hard for me to narrow the topic and focus on one thing. His work is so varied that every side of it is worth studying. In this research I want to make a kind of map of images and devices and so systematise the world of his videos. I want to understand how a whole and, above all, distinctive image comes together from the work of different directors: how it was made and what it consists of.

      How do you keep visual unity while working with different teams and shooting techniques? My hypothesis: there is a set of characteristic features from which a whole image reflecting the author’s inner world is built. I deliberately avoid searching for interpretations and hidden meanings — there are too many. Instead I focus on the main visual devices and narratives that shape the author’s distinctive image and convey his life experience and view of the world.

      I look at works from 2017–2024: from the famous “Panelka”, which became a cult piece of Russian “chthonic”, to the brand-new “Young Russian”, released just a month ago. I also analyse short films directed by Husky himself — “Lucifer” and “Psychotronics” — and “Ode to Nothing DVDRip”. In them I single out the shared features and details that add up to one image.

      The theoretical basis is Yuri Lotman’s “Semiotics of Cinema and Problems of Film Aesthetics” and Andrei Tarkovsky’s “Sculpting in Time”: these texts help to understand and analyse the nature of film images.

      I treat the artistic image and the symbol as the basis of the space-time reality of the videos. So the structure of the research follows the key elements of film language, sorted into categories — particular images and moods. I move from the signified to the signifier. The research has three parts, in which I analyse the main signs — the author’s messages. This approach helps to trace patterns and list the main tendencies in how my subject is portrayed.
  native:
    label: I. The Native
    body: |-
      Space

      The musician is often called a “Russian rapper” and compared to Sergei Yesenin. Throughout his career he has returned again and again to the theme of the homeland and his endless love for it.

      Dmitry Kuznetsov — Husky’s real name — was born and raised in Ulan-Ude, a city where different cultures and traditions intertwine. He spent his childhood in a village on the shore of Lake Baikal, playing street football with local kids. Those games and friendships strongly shaped his work.

      This line of his work reflects his childhood memories and important sides of his personality and view of the world.

    body: |-
      “Endless Shop”, 2021, Husky (dir. Dima Maseikin)

      “Endless Shop” uses many visual devices: a textured comic-book style, carefully designed sound, contrast of light and colour.

      Husky robots take over the Earth. The result is a dark dystopia about a peacekeeper robot that is meant to save a humanity mired in violence and ends up seizing the planet. The whole video is built on a backward camera fly-through across space and time — the world seems to assemble itself in front of the hero. The intro tells almost the entire plot, and it is the intro that creates the sense of postmodernism and amorphousness.
