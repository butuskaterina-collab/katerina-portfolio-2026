    label: About the project
    body: |-
      A project at the HSE School of Design, 2025. Object design, neural networks, 3D models.

      The project grew out of thinking about damage as a trace of time and watching how fragile things hold together — not perfectly, but enough to go on existing.

      Votive Objects is a series of intimate objects about damage as a trace of time and a gesture of care. Each object captures a moment of fragile balance: cracks and breaks stay visible, metal holds them, and that act is enough for the form to go on existing.

      The objects are not meant to be used the same way or placed symmetrically. What unites them is a logic of fragments, joining and holding: each object is a variation of the same gesture.

      Their scale and tactility invite a personal, intimate interaction, with nothing for show.

  tz:
    label: Production brief
    body: |-
      The Votive Objects series, 5 objects: interior, votive, tabletop. Small and medium scale: up to 160 mm on the longest side, from 45 mm in height. Decorative objects, not for contact with food.

      Materials. Main — photopolymer resin with a stone / ceramic look (white, milky): matte surface, porous stone-like texture, suitable for hand sanding and patination. Secondary — imitation liquid metal (epoxy resin with metal powder, cold casting).

      3D printing. Layer height 0.025–0.05 mm, solid models without moving parts, wall thickness 4–7 mm. Scale 1 : 1, no non-manifold geometry or intersections, minimum element thickness respected.

      Stage 1 — preparing the 3D models: geometry check, wall thickness control, orientation in the slicer.

      Stage 2 — printing on an SLA / DLP printer, minimal supports on visible surfaces.

      Stage 3 — post-processing: washing in isopropyl alcohol, removing supports, UV curing.

      Stage 4 — hand finishing: sanding, a “worn” texture, metal elements added by hand.

      Stage 5 — final finish: off-white patina, matte protective coating.

      The series is developed to the level of concept and prototypes. The technology can be adapted to a specific production, and variation in textures is part of the concept. It can move to other technologies — casting, ceramic production — without changing the logic of the form.
